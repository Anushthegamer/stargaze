Privacy Policy

StarGaze · last updated 31 August 2026

StarGaze does not collect, store, or transmit any personal data. There is no backend server, no account system, and no analytics of any kind. Everything below describes data the app reads on your device and immediately uses there.

Location

The app reads your latitude and longitude to work out which stars, planets and constellations are above the horizon where you are — this is a required input to the astronomy, not an optional extra. Your coordinates are used only for that calculation, are never sent anywhere, and are not retained after the session. If you decline location access, you can type coordinates in manually and the app works normally.

Camera

If you allow it, the app shows a live rear-camera feed behind the star overlay so you can line the overlay up against the real sky. Nothing is captured, recorded, stored, analysed, or transmitted — the feed is displayed and discarded frame by frame, and no image recognition is performed on it. The app is fully usable without camera access.

Motion and compass sensors

Device orientation readings are used to work out which way the phone is pointing. They are read continuously while the app is open and used only to aim the view. They are never transmitted or stored.

Stored on your device

The app saves a small amount of data locally, in your browser's own storage, so it remembers your preferences between visits:

  • Display settings (magnitude cutoff, field of view, which overlays are on)
  • Your compass calibration offset, and when and roughly where it was measured
  • A manually entered location, if you set one

This never leaves your device. Clearing the app's data (or the site's data in your browser) erases all of it.

Network access

The star catalogue, constellation figures, planetary data and magnetic model are bundled with the app and loaded from it directly — the app works fully offline, which is the point. Web fonts are the one exception: the browser fetches them from Google Fonts on first load, which is a normal request to that service and carries no data about you beyond what any web page request does. The Android build declares the internet permission because the Capacitor runtime it is built on requires it, not because the app makes network calls of its own.

Children

StarGaze collects no data from anyone, of any age.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated date above changes with it. The app's full source is public, so any claim on this page can be checked against the code that implements it.

Contact
